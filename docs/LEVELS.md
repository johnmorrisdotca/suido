# The levels in detail

How Suido's fixed levels are stored, read, proved and made, and the set of sixty-four with big pieces among the ordinary ones. Moved here from [the README's Levels section](../README.md#levels), which has the summary, to keep the README under the length npm shows.

## The levels by size

- **Each size is its own import**, loaded when asked for (`loadSuidoLevels("8x8")`), or directly as `@johnmorrisdotca/suido/levels-8x8`, so a page playing 5×5 carries none of the others. A size's file is 8 KB to 51 KB gzipped (5×5 to 14×14), and the huge ones are 26 KB (20×20), 49 KB (28×28) and about 60 KB (20×50) gzipped, for sixty-four levels each: a level is its board code, one digit for each cell for its answer, and its twists.
- **A row** is `[board, turns, twists]`: the board as a code, the answer as one digit a cell (the quarter turns clockwise from the way the board gives the piece to the way the answer has it), and the twists it declares, as kebab-case words (`"wrap locked"`; `""` for a plain level). `levelAnswer(row)` is the answer as a code, which `checkSuidoAnswer(row[0], answer)` accepts; `levelBoard(row)` and `levelSolution(row)` give the layout and the pieces of the answer; `declaredTwists(row)` the twists.
- **Proved on every build.** Each level is solved from scratch and must have exactly one answer, the stored one; its declared twists must be the twists its board has; no two are the same board turned or mirrored (`symmetryKey`); and the order, the marks and the lessons are checked against the measure.
- **Made on a desk**, never on a site: `node scripts/suido-levels.ts` makes a pool of boards of each kind from seeds taken from the size, the kind and a number (so the same run writes the same files), measures each, and takes the board nearest each level's aim that is no easier than the one before. About 18 minutes in all on one laptop, one process a size (7 s at 5×5 to 5 minutes at 14×14).

## The big-pieces levels

`loadSuidoBigLevels()` (from `@johnmorrisdotca/suido/levels`) fetches a set of **sixty-four levels with big pieces among the ordinary ones**, from the easiest to the hardest across every size from 5×5 to 20×20. Every level is a mix: ordinary 1×1 pieces with some 2×2 big pieces among them, never a board of big pieces alone. The data is its own entry, `@johnmorrisdotca/suido/levels-big`, and `@johnmorrisdotca/suido/levels-info` has everything about a level but its board (`suidoBigSize(level)`, `suidoBigScore(level)`, `suidoBigPieces(level)`, `suidoBigRole(level)`), so a server can name a level without carrying sixty-four boards.

- **A level is a level of its own size.** Level 12 of the set is a 5×5, level 64 a 20×20, and every size from 5×5 to 14×14 has some, with the long 5×7: 18 levels at 5×5, 1 at 5×7, 3 at 6×6, 3 at 7×7, 2 at 8×8, 3 at 9×9, 3 at 10×10, 2 at 11×11, 2 at 12×12, 2 at 13×13, 5 at 14×14 and 20 at 20×20. What the set keeps apart is its numbering, 1 to 64, in four blocks of sixteen that open one after another.
- **One score, 1 to 100, across every size.** A size's own difficulty (`difficultyOf`) ranks a board among boards of its size, so a 5×5 and a 20×20 can both be 80 and not be alike. The set's score (`bigScoreOf`, `docs/DIFFICULTY.md`) blends two things, half and half: how much of the board the answer uses (`coverageOf` is the share of pieces the water goes through, and `usedPiecesOf` counts them; every board of the set is a network, so that is all of them, from 25 to 400, on a log scale) and how tangled the board is for its size. Level 1 scores 1, level 64 scores 100, every level is no easier than the one before, and the steps between them are 1.6 on average and never more than 5.
- **Big pieces grow with the levels.** The first eighteen levels have one big piece each among 25 pieces; the 20×20s have 10 to 32. How tricky a plate is grows too: the early levels draw from the plain shapes (`bigKinds: "simple"`: one or two pipes, at most four openings), the middle ones from every shape of one or two pipes (`"more"`), and the hardest from all 699, three pipes in one plate included (`"all"`). In all, the 64 levels have 614 big pieces of 316 different shapes in 29 of the 32 families (181 with one pipe, 328 with two and 105 with three); `suidoBigPieces(level)` says how many a level has and the share of the board they cover.
- **The lessons are the first set's pattern.** The 15th level of a block teaches a twist and the 16th tests it: blocks that turn as one (levels 15 and 16), a second pump (31 and 32), walls (47 and 48) and edges that join (63 and 64); the other levels of a later block mix in the ones already taught.
- **Proved on every build** as the first set's are: each is solved from scratch and has exactly one answer, no two are the same board turned or mirrored, and the score, the sizes, the twists and the big pieces each level's row declares are read again from its board.
- **A row's answer for a square.** A big piece or a block turns as a whole and its pieces move round it, so an answer is not a turn for each cell there: the square's quarter turns are written at its top left cell, with 0 at the other three (`turnsOf`, `levelSolution`).
- **Made on a desk**, as the first set: `node scripts/suido-big-levels.ts` makes a pool of boards of each kind a place can be at each size, scores them across the set, and takes for each of the sixty-four places the board nearest its aim (1 to 100 in even steps) that is no easier than the one before. About fifteen minutes on one laptop.
